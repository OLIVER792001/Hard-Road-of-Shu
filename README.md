# Hard Road of Shu · Digital Humanities Project

## Project Overview
A digital exploration of Li Bai’s (701–762 CE) iconic Tang Dynasty poem *Hard Road of Shu* (蜀道难), this project bridges classical Chinese literature with modern digital scholarship. By integrating bilingual texts, historical context, manuscript archives, and interactive geography, it offers an immersive, accessible experience for global readers to engage with one of China’s greatest literary masterpieces.

## Core Features
- **Bilingual Parallel Reader**: Line-by-line comparison of the original Chinese poem (with Song Dynasty annotations) and English translation (adapted from Witter Bynner’s authoritative version).
- **Manuscript Viewer**: High-resolution scan of a Southern Song Dynasty annotated manuscript (collated by Yang Qixian and Xiao Shiyun), with drag-to-pan and zoom functionality.
- **Textual Collation**: Visual markup of variant readings (deleted text struck through, revised readings in red superscript) for scholarly reference.
- **Interactive Map**: Geographic visualization of key locations referenced in the poem (e.g., Emei Mountain, Jianmen Pass), linking verses to historical and modern place names.
- **Contextual Essays**: In-depth analysis of Li Bai’s life, the poem’s political backdrop (Tianbao era crises), and its literary significance in Chinese poetry.

## How to Use
1. Visit the project homepage:  
   https://oliver792001.github.io/Hard-Road-of-Shu/
2. Explore the bilingual text: Scroll through the parallel poem with original Chinese and English translation.
3. Examine the manuscript: Drag to navigate and use the +/- buttons to zoom in on annotations.
4. Interact with the map: Click on location markers to discover their connection to the poem’s imagery.
5. Dive into context: Read about Li Bai’s biography, the Tang Dynasty historical setting, and the poem’s literary impact.

## Project Significance
This project aims to **digitally revitalize classical literature** by making Tang poetry interactive and cross-culturally accessible. It serves as a resource for:
- Students and educators of Chinese literature, history, and digital humanities
- Scholars researching Tang poetry, textual criticism, or literary geography
- Global enthusiasts interested in exploring Chinese cultural heritage through modern technology
